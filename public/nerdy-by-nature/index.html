Candidate guide

Nerdy by Nature

A candidate's guide to our hiring process.

We believe the best hiring process is a transparent one. This guide walks you through exactly what to expect when you interview at Nerdy — from first contact to offer — and what we're looking for at each stage.

We also want to be upfront about something: we move fast here, and our interviews reflect that. We're not interested in trick questions or whiteboard theater. We're looking for people who can think clearly, build things, and work with AI as a genuine collaborator.

BEFORE YOU APPLY

Read the job description carefully. Then read it again. We write our job descriptions to be specific — if we say AI-native, we mean it. If we list a stack, we're using it daily.

A few things worth doing before you apply:

  • Review our investor relations page at investors.nerdy.com to understand the business and where we are financially.
  • Read the most recent shareholder letter. Chuck's letters are candid and detailed — they tell you what we're building, what's working, and what isn't.
  • Try Varsity Tutors as a learner or tutor if you haven't. Understanding the product you're building for matters here.
  • Review your AI tooling. We'll ask how you actually use AI in your work — be ready to be specific.

OUR HIRING PROCESS

Every role is a little different, but most engineering and product roles follow this path:

STEP 1: APPLICATION REVIEW

1–3 business days

A human reads every application. We don't auto-reject based on keywords. We're looking for signal in your experience — not credential-matching.

Callout — What we look at Specificity in your resume. What did you actually build? What was the outcome? Generic descriptions of responsibilities don't tell us much. Concrete descriptions of shipped things do.

STEP 2: RECRUITER SCREEN

30 minutes

A short call with someone from our talent team. This is a two-way conversation — we'll tell you more about the role and you should ask us hard questions.

We'll cover:

  • Your background and what you're looking for
  • Why Nerdy, why now
  • Compensation expectations and logistics
  • Your current AI workflow and tools
Callout — Tip Come with questions. Candidates who don't ask questions at this stage rarely make it to the next one. We want people who are curious and discerning about where they work.

STEP 3: HIRING MANAGER INTERVIEW

45–60 minutes

A deeper conversation with the person you'd report to. Expect questions about how you think, how you've approached hard problems, and how you work with others.

We use the STAR method for behavioral questions. Prepare 3–5 stories from your experience that demonstrate:

  • Owning something end-to-end under ambiguity
  • Moving fast without sacrificing quality
  • Using AI as a genuine force multiplier — not just a search engine
  • Disagreeing with someone and changing their mind (or being changed)
Callout — On AI use We actively encourage AI use during interviews unless specifically told otherwise. If you use Claude or ChatGPT to help you prep, tell us. We're more interested in how you use AI than whether you use it.

STEP 4: TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT OR CASE STUDY

This varies by role:

  • Engineering: A take-home or live coding assessment focused on real problems, not toy puzzles. Expect to use your normal tools including AI coding assistants.
  • Product: A case study or product critique. We'll give you a real problem space and ask how you'd approach it.
  • Sales/GTM: A mock discovery call or pipeline review.
  • Operations: A workflow design or process audit exercise.

Time expectations: we try to keep assessments under 2–3 hours of actual work. If something feels like it's taking much longer, reach out to your recruiter.

STEP 5: FINAL LOOP

2–3 hours, usually virtual

Interviews with 2–4 people across the team. Each interviewer has a specific focus area so you won't repeat yourself endlessly.

You'll typically meet:

  • A peer on the team
  • A cross-functional stakeholder
  • A senior leader (VP or above) for select roles
Callout — What we're calibrating Raising the bar. We ask ourselves: is this person better than the median of who we'd hire for this role? If the answer isn't clearly yes, we don't extend an offer. That's about maintaining a team where everyone makes everyone else better.

STEP 6: OFFER AND REFERENCE CHECKS

If we're moving forward, you'll receive an offer within 1–2 business days of your final loop. We run reference checks in parallel — we'll ask for 2–3 references, ideally people you've worked with directly.

Our offers include base salary, equity (RSUs), and benefits. We're transparent about comp ranges — if you haven't discussed numbers yet, ask your recruiter.

OUR PRINCIPLES, APPLIED TO HIRING

1. AI-NATIVE

We're not looking for people who have "experimented with AI." We're looking for people who have fundamentally changed how they work because of AI. Be specific about this. The vaguer your answer, the more it signals surface-level adoption.

2. FOUNDER VELOCITY

Can you move from idea to shipped in days, not months? The most compelling candidates have a story about something they built faster than anyone expected.

3. FULL-STACK OWNERSHIP

We want to hear about things you owned end-to-end. Not things you contributed to. Things you were personally accountable for, from design through operation.

4. CONTRIBUTION-BASED REWARD

We're looking for people motivated by impact, not process or politics. If your best work stories are about navigating organizational complexity rather than building things, we're probably not the right fit for each other.

FAQs

Can I use AI during the interview or assessment?

Yes, unless we specifically ask you not to. We'll always be clear. If you use AI during an assessment, note how and where — that context is useful to us.

How long does the process take?

For most roles, start to offer in 2–3 weeks. We move faster for urgent roles. If you're on a timeline, tell your recruiter up front.

Do you offer visa sponsorship?

It depends on the role and level. Ask your recruiter early.

Are roles remote?

Most engineering and product roles are remote-friendly. Some sales and operations roles have location preferences. Check the specific job description.

What's the compensation philosophy?

We pay at or above market. Top performers in their first year regularly out-earn peers at other companies. Equity is a meaningful part of the package for senior roles.

A NOTE ON FEEDBACK

We try to give meaningful feedback to every candidate who makes it to the hiring manager stage. If you don't hear from us within a week of your final interview, follow up with your recruiter.

We know interviewing is time-consuming and sometimes stressful. We appreciate you considering Nerdy, and we'll do our best to make the process worth your time — regardless of the outcome.
